1 · Angles in radians

Instead of degrees, you can measure an angle as the length of the arc on the unit circle (radius 1). The whole circle has circumference \(2\pi\), so:

$$360^\circ = 2\pi, \qquad 180^\circ = \pi, \qquad 90^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$$

That's all you need to memorise. Work everything else out from \(180^\circ = \pi\): divide by 180, multiply by π (or the other way round).

Try it yourself: what is 60° in radians?

\(60^\circ = \frac{60}{180}\pi = \frac{\pi}{3}\)

2 · The unit circle: cos, sin, tan

Turn a ray by the angle α (anticlockwise, starting from the positive x-axis). It meets the circle at the point \(P_\alpha\). Then:

  • cos(α) = the x-coordinate of P (how far right/left)
  • sin(α) = the y-coordinate of P (how far up/down)
  • tan(α) = the slope of the ray, i.e. \(\frac{\sin(\alpha)}{\cos(\alpha)}\). Exactly the m from \(\m x + \q\)! You can see it on the line \(x = 1\).
α

Try it out: drag P around the unit circle →

Two things you can see straight from the picture:

  • cos and sin are always between −1 and 1. P is on a circle of radius 1, after all.
  • Pythagoras: the triangle made of cos, sin and radius 1 is right-angled, so \(\sin^2(\alpha) + \cos^2(\alpha) = 1\).

3 · The recipes

  1. Degrees ↔ radians\(r = \frac{\pi}{180}\cdot d, \quad d = \frac{180}{\pi}\cdot r\)Always think from \(180^\circ = \pi\).
  2. SignsIn which quarter (quadrant) does P lie?cos + on the right, sin + at the top. tan + when both have the same sign (I and III).
  3. Equation \(\sin(\alpha) = c\)horizontal line \(y = c\), meets the circle twice\(\alpha_1\) from the calculator, \(\alpha_2 = 180^\circ - \alpha_1\). Then \(+\,k\cdot 360^\circ\).
  4. Equation \(\cos(\alpha) = c\)vertical line \(x = c\), meets the circle twice\(\alpha_1\) from the calculator, \(\alpha_2 = -\alpha_1\). Then \(+\,k\cdot 360^\circ\).
  5. Equation \(\tan(\alpha) = c\)line through O with slope c\(\alpha_1\) from the calculator, then \(+\,k\cdot 180^\circ\) (the line passes through two opposite points).
  6. One known → the other two\(\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1\) and \(\tan = \frac{\sin}{\cos}\). Signs from the quadrant.

E1

(a) Convert the angles 0°, 360°, 180°, 90°, 60°, 30°, −90°, 45°, 1° exactly into radians, and \(1\) rad into degrees. (b) Find the conversion functions \(d(r)\) (radians → degrees) and \(r(d)\) (degrees → radians).
  1. STEP 1

    The one thing you need to know

    Half a circle: half the circumference of the unit circle is π.

    $$180^\circ = \pi$$
  2. STEP 2

    Degrees → radians: divide by 180, multiply by π

    How many “180°” is it? That many π.

    $$\begin{aligned} 360^\circ &= 2\pi & 90^\circ &= \tfrac{90}{180}\pi = \tfrac{\pi}{2} & 60^\circ &= \tfrac{\pi}{3} \\ 30^\circ &= \tfrac{\pi}{6} & -90^\circ &= -\tfrac{\pi}{2} & 45^\circ &= \tfrac{\pi}{4} \\ 1^\circ &= \tfrac{\pi}{180} & 0^\circ &= 0 \end{aligned}$$
  3. STEP 3

    Radians → degrees: divide by π, multiply by 180

    $$1 = \tfrac{180^\circ}{\pi} \approx 57.3^\circ$$
  4. STEP 4

    (b) Write it as functions

    The calculations from steps 2 and 3, just with a letter instead of a number: r = angle in radians, d = angle in degrees.

    $$d(r) = \frac{180}{\pi}\cdot r, \qquad r(d) = \frac{\pi}{180}\cdot d$$

    Check: \(d(\pi) = 180\) ✓, \(r(180) = \pi\) ✓

Only remember \(180^\circ = \pi\). Work out the rest each time.

E2

Draw a unit circle with a diameter of 8 cm. (a)–(c) Mark \(P_{55^\circ}, P_{290^\circ}, P_{-190^\circ}, P_{380^\circ}\), measure cos, sin and tan and check with the calculator. (d)–(h) For which α is \(\sin(\alpha) = 0.8\), \(\cos(\alpha) = -0.2\), \(\tan(\alpha) = -2\), \(\sin(\alpha) = 4.2\), \(\cos(\alpha) = -2\)?
  1. TIP

    Measure in units, not in cm

    8 cm diameter = 4 cm radius = 1 unit. So divide the measured cm by 4. In the unit circle above you can set any angle and compare.

  2. (a)(b)

    Read off cos = x, sin = y

    Careful: 380° = 360° + 20°, the same point as 20°. −190° means: 190° clockwise.

    $$\begin{aligned} \cos 55^\circ &\approx 0.574, & \sin 55^\circ &\approx 0.819 \\ \cos 290^\circ &\approx 0.342, & \sin 290^\circ &\approx -0.940 \\ \cos(-190^\circ) &\approx -0.985, & \sin(-190^\circ) &\approx 0.174 \\ \cos 380^\circ &\approx 0.940, & \sin 380^\circ &\approx 0.342 \end{aligned}$$
  3. (c)

    Read off tan on the line x = 1

    $$\begin{aligned} \tan 55^\circ &\approx 1.428 & \tan 290^\circ &\approx -2.747 \\ \tan(-190^\circ) &\approx -0.176 & \tan 380^\circ &\approx 0.364 \end{aligned}$$
  4. (d)

    \(\sin(\alpha) = 0.8\)

    Draw the horizontal line y = 0.8. It meets the circle twice, mirrored left and right.

    $$\alpha_1 \approx 53.1^\circ, \quad \alpha_2 = 180^\circ - 53.1^\circ \approx 126.9^\circ, \qquad \alpha = \alpha_{1,2} + k\cdot 360^\circ,\ k \in \mathbb{Z}$$

    \(k\cdot 360^\circ\): every full turn more or less lands on the same point again. That's why there are infinitely many solutions.

  5. (e)

    \(\cos(\alpha) = -0.2\)

    Vertical line x = −0.2. The two points are mirrored top and bottom.

    $$\alpha \approx \pm 101.5^\circ + k\cdot 360^\circ \quad(\text{so also } 258.5^\circ)$$
  6. (f)

    \(\tan(\alpha) = -2\)

    Line through O with slope −2. It meets the circle at two opposite points, 180° apart.

    $$\alpha \approx 116.6^\circ + k\cdot 180^\circ$$

    The calculator gives \(-63.4^\circ\): the same family (\(-63.4^\circ + 180^\circ = 116.6^\circ\)).

  7. (g)(h)

    \(\sin(\alpha) = 4.2\), \(\cos(\alpha) = -2\)

    No solution: cos and sin are always between −1 and 1.

E3

For each angle interval, say whether cos, sin and tan are positive (+) or negative (−). No calculator, use the unit circle.
  1. STEP 1

    cos = x

    P to the right of the y-axis → cos positive. That's between −90° and 90°.

  2. STEP 2

    sin = y

    P above the x-axis → sin positive. That's between 0° and 180°.

  3. STEP 3

    tan = sin / cos

    Same signs divided → +, different → −. So tan is positive in quadrants I and III.

  4. STEP 4

    Fill in the table

    Negative angles: count clockwise. \((-180^\circ, -90^\circ)\) is the same quarter as \((180^\circ, 270^\circ)\).

    Interval(0°, 90°)(90°, 180°)(180°, 270°)(270°, 360°)(−180°, −90°)(−90°, 0°)
    QuadrantIIIIIIIVIIIIV
    cos+−−+−+
    sin++−−−−
    tan+−+−+−
Memory aid: in quadrant I everything is positive, in II only sin, in III only tan, in IV only cos.

E4

(a) Give the vertices of a regular pentagon ABCDE on the unit circle exactly, with \(A = (1, 0)\). (b) The same on a circle of radius 4 cm, rounded to two decimals. (c) Vertices of a regular n-gon with circumradius r?
  1. STEP 1

    How far apart are the vertices?

    5 equal pieces of the full circle.

    $$360^\circ : 5 = 72^\circ$$
  2. (a)

    Each vertex is a point \(P_\alpha = (\cos\alpha, \sin\alpha)\)

    $$\begin{aligned} A &= (\cos 0^\circ, \sin 0^\circ) = (1, 0) & B &= (\cos 72^\circ, \sin 72^\circ) \\ C &= (\cos 144^\circ, \sin 144^\circ) & D &= (\cos 216^\circ, \sin 216^\circ) \\ E &= (\cos 288^\circ, \sin 288^\circ) \end{aligned}$$
  3. (b)

    Radius 4: multiply everything by 4

    A circle 4 times bigger stretches every point by a factor of 4.

    $$\begin{aligned} A &= (4, 0) & B &\approx (1.24,\ 3.80) \\ C &\approx (-3.24,\ 2.35) & D &\approx (-3.24,\ -2.35) \\ E &\approx (1.24,\ -3.80) \end{aligned}$$
  4. (c)

    In general: n vertices, radius r

    Spacing \(\frac{360^\circ}{n}\), vertex number k at the angle \(k\cdot\frac{360^\circ}{n}\), everything times r.

    $$\left(r\cos\!\left(k\cdot\tfrac{360^\circ}{n}\right),\ r\sin\!\left(k\cdot\tfrac{360^\circ}{n}\right)\right), \qquad k = 0, 1, \dots, n-1$$

E5

Find cos, sin and tan of 30° and 45° exactly, without a calculator. Then make a table for all multiples of 30° and 45° between 0° and 360°.
  1. STEP 1

    30°: half an equilateral triangle

    If you mirror the triangle O, P, foot point in the x-axis, you get an equilateral triangle with side 1 (all angles 60°). The y-coordinate of P is half of that.

    $$\sin 30^\circ = \tfrac12$$
  2. STEP 2

    cos 30° with Pythagoras

    $$\begin{aligned}\cos 30^\circ &= \sqrt{1 - \left(\tfrac12\right)^2} = \sqrt{\tfrac34} = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} \\ \tan 30^\circ &= \frac{1/2}{\sqrt3/2} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3} = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{3}\end{aligned}$$
  3. STEP 3

    45°: half a square

    At 45°, x and y are the same length. Pythagoras: \(x^2 + x^2 = 1\).

    $$\begin{aligned}\cos 45^\circ = \sin 45^\circ &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} = \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2} \\ \tan 45^\circ &= 1\end{aligned}$$
  4. STEP 4

    60°: like 30°, with x and y swapped

    $$\begin{aligned}\cos 60^\circ &= \tfrac12 \\ \sin 60^\circ &= \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} \\ \tan 60^\circ &= \sqrt3\end{aligned}$$
  5. STEP 5

    All other angles: mirror + signs

    For example 150° = 180° − 30°: the point is the one for 30°, mirrored to the left. Same numbers, but cos becomes negative (quadrant II). Signs as in E3.

    α0°30°45°60°90°120°135°150°180°210°225°240°270°300°315°330°
    cos(α)\(1\)\(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\)\(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)\(\tfrac{1}{2}\)\(0\)\(- \tfrac{1}{2}\)\(- \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)\(- \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\)\(-1\)\(- \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\)\(- \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)\(- \tfrac{1}{2}\)\(0\)\(\tfrac{1}{2}\)\(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)\(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\)

Memory aid for the sin row at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°: \(\frac{\sqrt0}{2}, \frac{\sqrt1}{2}, \frac{\sqrt2}{2}, \frac{\sqrt3}{2}, \frac{\sqrt4}{2}\). The cos row is the same, backwards.

E6

(a) Which of the values \(\pm\sin(\pm\alpha)\), \(\pm\cos(\pm\alpha)\) are equal? (b) Which of \(\sin\alpha\), \(\sin(\alpha + 90^\circ)\), \(-\sin(\alpha + 180^\circ)\), \(\sin(\alpha + 360^\circ)\), \(\cos\alpha\), \(-\cos(\alpha + 90^\circ)\), \(-\cos(\alpha + 180^\circ)\), \(\cos(\alpha + 360^\circ)\) are equal? (c) What about \(\tan\alpha\), \(\sin^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha\), 1, \(\frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}\)?
  1. TIP

    Draw, don't calculate

    Draw \(P_\alpha\) and the turned or mirrored point. Then compare x and y. In the unit circle above you can set e.g. 30° and then −30°.

  2. (a)

    −α: mirrored in the x-axis

    x stays the same, y changes sign.

    $$\cos(-\alpha) = \cos(\alpha), \qquad \sin(-\alpha) = -\sin(\alpha)$$
  3. (b)

    Turn by +90°, +180°, +360°

    +360°: same point. +180°: the opposite point, both coordinates change sign. +90°: (x, y) becomes (−y, x).

    $$\begin{aligned} \sin\alpha &= -\sin(\alpha + 180^\circ) \\ &= \sin(\alpha + 360^\circ) \\ &= -\cos(\alpha + 90^\circ) \\[6pt] \cos\alpha &= \sin(\alpha + 90^\circ) \\ &= -\cos(\alpha + 180^\circ) \\ &= \cos(\alpha + 360^\circ) \end{aligned}$$
  4. (c)

    Two pairs

    $$\tan\alpha = \frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha} \quad(\text{definition}), \qquad \sin^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha = 1 \quad(\text{Pythagoras})$$

E7

If you know one of the numbers \(\cos\alpha\), \(\sin\alpha\), \(\tan\alpha\), you can find the other two up to their sign. Use \(\tan\alpha = \frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}\) and \(\sin^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha = 1\). (a) cos known, (b) sin known, (c) tan known.
  1. (a)

    cos known → sin

    Solve Pythagoras for \(\sin\). The square root gives two possibilities: ±.

    $$\sin^2\alpha = 1 - \cos^2\alpha \iff \sin\alpha = \pm\sqrt{1 - \cos^2\alpha}$$
  2. (a)

    cos known → tan

    $$\tan\alpha = \frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha} = \pm\frac{\sqrt{1 - \cos^2\alpha}}{\cos\alpha}$$
  3. (a)

    Signs for \(\alpha \in \left(\frac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right)\)

    That's 90° to 180°, so quadrant II: sin positive, cos negative, tan negative.

    $$\sin\alpha = +\sqrt{1 - \cos^2\alpha}, \qquad \tan\alpha = \frac{\sqrt{1 - \cos^2\alpha}}{\cos\alpha}\ (\text{negative, because } \cos\alpha \lt 0)$$

    Example: \(\cos\alpha = -\frac35\) → \(\sin\alpha = \sqrt{1 - \frac{9}{25}} = \frac45\), \(\tan\alpha = \frac{4/5}{-3/5} = -\frac43\).

  4. (b)

    sin known

    Exactly the same, with sin and cos swapped.

    $$\cos\alpha = \pm\sqrt{1 - \sin^2\alpha}, \qquad \tan\alpha = \pm\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sqrt{1 - \sin^2\alpha}}$$
  5. (c)

    tan known

    Trick: substitute \(\sin\alpha = \tan\alpha\cdot\cos\alpha\) into Pythagoras. Then only cos is left.

    $$\begin{aligned} \tan^2\alpha\cdot\cos^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha &= 1 \\ \cos^2\alpha\,(\tan^2\alpha + 1) &= 1 \\ \cos\alpha &= \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \tan^2\alpha}} \\ \sin\alpha &= \tan\alpha\cdot\cos\alpha \\ &= \pm\frac{\tan\alpha}{\sqrt{1 + \tan^2\alpha}} \end{aligned}$$
