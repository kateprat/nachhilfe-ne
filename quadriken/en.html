1 · What is a parabola?

Conics (Quadriken) are curves whose equation contains x and y at most squared: parabolas, circles, ellipses and hyperbolas. This chapter is about parabolas and circles.

The definition (17.1.1)

Given a point F, the focus, and a line ℓ, the directrix. The parabola is the set of all points P that are the same distance from F and from ℓ:

$$\overline{PF} = \overline{P\ell}$$

Drag F, the directrix or P in the picture: the red and the blue segment always stay the same length.

From the definition to the function

The example from the script: \(F = (0, 1)\), ℓ is the x-axis. For a point \(P = (x, y)\), “same distance” means:

$$\begin{aligned} \overline{P\ell} &= \overline{PF} \\ |y| &= \sqrt{x^2 + (y - 1)^2} \quad\mid\ (\ )^2 \\ y^2 &= x^2 + y^2 - 2y + 1 \\ 2y &= x^2 + 1 \\ y &= \tfrac12 x^2 + \tfrac12 \end{aligned}$$

So the parabola is the graph of a quadratic function. That is no coincidence:

Theorem 17.1.3

The graph of every quadratic function \(f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c\) with \(a \neq 0\) is a parabola.

Vertex form

\(f(x) = a(x - u)^2 + v\). From it you read off directly:

  • the vertex is \(S = (u, v)\), the lowest (or highest) point;
  • \(a > 0\): opens upwards, \(a < 0\): downwards; large \(|a|\): narrow, small \(|a|\): wide;
  • for \(f(x) = ax^2\) the focus is at \(F = \left(0, \tfrac{1}{4a}\right)\) and the directrix at \(y = -\tfrac{1}{4a}\) (17.1.2).

2 · What do the letters mean?

A letter stands for a number that we don't know yet, or that is allowed to change. That's all. You calculate with it exactly like with a number.

Substituting into x

\(f(x) = x^2 - 2x + 3\) is a rule: “Take a number x and calculate with it.” \(f(2)\) means: write 2 everywhere you see x.

$$\begin{aligned} f(2) &= 2^2 - 2\cdot 2 + 3 \\ &= 4 - 4 + 3 = 3 \end{aligned}$$

Try it yourself: what is \(f(-1)\)?

\(f(-1) = (-1)^2 - 2\cdot(-1) + 3 = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6\). Careful: brackets around negative numbers!

Two kinds of letters: variable and parameters

  • x is the variable. It moves: x stands for “any position on the x-axis”. Each x gives a different function value.
  • \(\m\) and \(\q\) are parameters. For one particular line they are fixed numbers, we just don't know them yet. These are exactly the numbers we are looking for in the tangent exercises.

The line \(g(x) = \m\cdot x + \q\)

m = slope: go 1 to the right and the line goes m up (down if m is negative).
q = y-intercept: where the line crosses the y-axis, at the point \((0, \q)\).

Move the sliders: m turns the line, q moves it up and down.

Does a point lie on the line? → Substitute

A point \(P = (x_P, y_P)\) lies on g if \(g(x_P) = y_P\) is true.

Example: \(g(x) = -3x + 5\) and \(P = (2, -1)\): \(g(2) = -3\cdot 2 + 5 = -1\). ✓ P lies on g.

Does \((1, 1)\) lie on g?

\(g(1) = -3 + 5 = 2 \neq 1\). No.

When m and q are still unknown

Then substituting a point gives an equation between m and q. We solve it for q. This is step 1 in almost every exercise.

$$P = (1,-3) \text{ lies on } g:\qquad \m\cdot 1 + \q = -3 \iff \q = -\m - 3$$

Setting equal and the discriminant

Where parabola f and line g meet, they are at the same height: \(f(x) = g(x)\). This gives a quadratic equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\). Its discriminant \(D = b^2 - 4ac\) tells you how many meeting points there are (chapter 14):

  • \(D \gt 0\): two intersection points. The line cuts the parabola.
  • \(D = 0\): exactly one point. The line is a tangent. Then \(x = -\frac{b}{2a}\).
  • \(D \lt 0\): no point. The line misses.

3 · The recipe for tangents

Almost all exercises A2–A8 use the same 7 steps. Only step 1 changes, depending on what is given.

  1. Set-up (Ansatz)\(t(x) = \m x + \q\)Substitute what is given.Slope given → substitute m. Point given → substitute it and solve for q.
  2. Set equal\(f(x) = t(x)\)Where do parabola and line meet?
  3. Standard form\(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\)Everything on one side, ordered by x², x, number. Read off a, b, c.
  4. Discriminant = 0\(b^2 - 4ac = 0\)Tangent means: exactly one point of contact.
  5. Solve for the number you are looking for (q or m)Often another quadratic equation → quadratic formula.
  6. Write down the tangentPut the numbers you found into \(t(x) = \m x + \q\).
  7. Point of contact\(x = -\frac{b}{2a}\), then \(y = t(x)\)With D = 0 the quadratic formula gives only this one solution.

How to write it down in the exam

Use the words from the script: “Ansatz: …”, “Since P lies on t, …”, “⇔”, “So …”, “The tangent we are looking for is …”. No calculator: leave fractions as they are. Check: substitute the point of contact into f as well, you must get the same y.

A1 (a)

Describe the parabola with focus \(F = (2, 1)\) and directrix \(\ell: y = -1\) as the graph of a function.
A parabola is the set of all points P that are equally far from F and from ℓ (17.1.1).
  1. STEP 1

    A general point

    Take any point \(P = (x, y)\) and write down when it lies on the parabola.

    $$\overline{P\ell} = \overline{PF}$$
  2. STEP 2

    Both distances as formulas

    Distance to a horizontal line: just the difference of the y-values. Distance to a point: Pythagoras.

    $$\overline{P\ell} = |y - (-1)| = |y + 1|, \qquad \overline{PF} = \sqrt{(x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2}$$
  3. STEP 3

    Set equal and square

    Both sides are not negative, so we may square. Then the root and the absolute value disappear.

    $$(y + 1)^2 = (x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2$$
  4. STEP 4

    Expand, \(y^2\) cancels

    $$\begin{aligned} y^2 + 2y + 1 &= (x - 2)^2 + y^2 - 2y + 1 \\ 4y &= (x - 2)^2 \end{aligned}$$
  5. STEP 5

    Solve for y

    $$y = \tfrac14 (x - 2)^2$$

    Check: the vertex \((2, 0)\) lies exactly halfway between \(F = (2, 1)\) and \(\ell: y = -1\) ✓

The parabola is the graph of \(f(x) = \frac14 (x - 2)^2\). Check it in the picture: F at (2, 1), ℓ at y = −1.

A1 (b) and (c)

(b) Let \(a \neq 0\) (you may assume \(a \gt 0\)). Describe the parabola with \(F = (0, a)\) and \(\ell: y = -a\) as the graph of a function.
(c) Find F and ℓ so that the parabola is the graph of \(f(x) = x^2\).
  1. (b)

    Exactly like (a), with a instead of numbers

    a is a parameter: a fixed number that we don't specify.

    $$\begin{aligned} (y + a)^2 &= x^2 + (y - a)^2 \\ y^2 + 2ay + a^2 &= x^2 + y^2 - 2ay + a^2 \\ 4ay &= x^2 \\ y &= \tfrac{1}{4a}\,x^2 \end{aligned}$$
  2. (c)

    Use (b) backwards

    We need \(\frac{1}{4a} = 1\).

    $$\tfrac{1}{4a} = 1 \iff a = \tfrac14, \qquad F = \left(0, \tfrac14\right),\quad \ell: y = -\tfrac14$$
This is 17.1.2 in the script: the graph of \(f(x) = \frac{1}{4a}x^2\) is the parabola with \(F = (0, a)\) and \(\ell: y = -a\).

A2

Find the tangent with slope −3 to the parabola \(f(x) = x^2 - 2x + 3\) and its point of contact.
  1. STEP 1

    Set-up

    The slope is given, so \(\m = -3\). Only \(\q\) is still unknown.

    $$t(x) = -3x + \q$$
  2. STEP 2

    Set equal

    Where line and parabola meet, they are at the same height.

    $$-3x + \q = x^2 - 2x + 3$$
  3. STEP 3

    Standard form

    Bring everything to the side with x² (there x² is positive), then sort.

    $$\begin{aligned} 0 &= x^2 - 2x + 3x + 3 - \q \\ 0 &= x^2 + 1\cdot x + (3 - \q) \end{aligned}$$

    So \(a = 1\), \(b = 1\), \(c = 3 - \q\).

  4. STEP 4

    Discriminant = 0

    Tangent = exactly one point of contact = exactly one solution = D is 0.

    $$D = b^2 - 4ac = 1^2 - 4\cdot 1\cdot(3 - \q) = 0$$
  5. STEP 5

    Solve for q

    Expand the bracket: \(-4\cdot 3 = -12\) and \(-4\cdot(-\q) = +4\q\).

    $$\begin{aligned} 1 - 12 + 4\q &= 0 \\ 4\q &= 11 \\ \q &= \tfrac{11}{4} \end{aligned}$$
  6. STEP 6

    Write down the tangent

    $$t(x) = -3x + \tfrac{11}{4}$$
  7. STEP 7

    Point of contact

    From step 3: a = 1, b = 1. With D = 0, \(x = -\frac{b}{2a}\). Then substitute x into t.

    $$x = -\frac{1}{2\cdot 1} = -\tfrac12, \qquad y = t\!\left(-\tfrac12\right) = \tfrac32 + \tfrac{11}{4} = \tfrac{17}{4}$$

    Check in f: \(f(-\tfrac12) = \tfrac14 + 1 + 3 = \tfrac{17}{4}\) ✓

The tangent is \(t(x) = -3x + \frac{11}{4}\), point of contact \(P = \left(-\frac12, \frac{17}{4}\right) = (-0.5;\ 4.25)\). Check it in the picture (example “A2”).

A3

Find all tangents to the parabola \(f(x) = x^2 - 2x + \frac{25}{16}\) that pass through the origin; give the point of contact for each.
  1. STEP 1

    Set-up

    The tangent passes through \((0, 0)\). Substitute: \(t(0) = 0\). That settles \(\q\); now \(\m\) is what we're looking for.

    $$\m\cdot 0 + \q = 0 \iff \q = 0, \qquad\text{so}\qquad t(x) = \m x$$
  2. STEP 2

    Set equal

    $$\m x = x^2 - 2x + \tfrac{25}{16}$$
  3. STEP 3

    Standard form

    Move \(\m x\) to the right and combine the two x-terms: \(-2x - \m x = (-2 - \m)\,x\). Brackets around b, because m is inside.

    $$0 = x^2 + (-2 - \m)\,x + \tfrac{25}{16}$$

    So \(a = 1\), \(b = -2 - \m\), \(c = \frac{25}{16}\).

  4. STEP 4

    Discriminant = 0

    $$D = (-2 - \m)^2 - 4\cdot 1\cdot \tfrac{25}{16} = (-2-\m)^2 - \tfrac{25}{4} = 0$$
  5. STEP 5

    Solve for m

    \((-2-\m)^2 = 4 + 4\m + \m^2\) (binomial formula). Multiply by 4 to get rid of the fraction. Then the quadratic formula, this time for m.

    $$\begin{aligned} \m^2 + 4\m + 4 - \tfrac{25}{4} &= 0 \quad \mid \cdot 4 \\ 4\m^2 + 16\m - 9 &= 0 \\ \m_{1,2} &= \frac{-16 \pm \sqrt{256 + 144}}{8} \\ &= \frac{-16 \pm 20}{8} \\ \m_1 = \tfrac12, &\quad \m_2 = -\tfrac92 \end{aligned}$$

    Two values for m → two tangents. This happens when the point lies outside the parabola.

  6. STEP 6

    Write down the tangents

    $$t_1(x) = \tfrac12 x, \qquad t_2(x) = -\tfrac92 x$$
  7. STEP 7

    Points of contact

    \(x = -\frac{b}{2a}\) with \(b = -2 - \m\) gives \(x = \frac{2 + \m}{2}\). Substitute each m separately.

    $$\begin{aligned} \m_1 = \tfrac12: &\quad x = \tfrac{2 + 1/2}{2} = \tfrac54, \quad y = \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac54 = \tfrac58 \\ \m_2 = -\tfrac92: &\quad x = \tfrac{2 - 9/2}{2} = -\tfrac54, \quad y = -\tfrac92\cdot\left(-\tfrac54\right) = \tfrac{45}{8} \end{aligned}$$
\(t_1(x) = \frac12 x\) with \(P_1 = \left(\frac54, \frac58\right) = (1.25;\ 0.625)\) and \(t_2(x) = -\frac92 x\) with \(P_2 = \left(-\frac54, \frac{45}{8}\right) = (-1.25;\ 5.625)\).

A4 (a)

Find the equation \(t(x) = \m x + \q\) of the tangent to \(f(x) = x^2\) at the point \(Q = \left(\frac12, \frac14\right)\).
  1. STEP 1

    Set-up

    Q lies on t, so \(t(\tfrac12) = \tfrac14\). Solve for q.

    $$\m\cdot\tfrac12 + \q = \tfrac14 \iff \q = \tfrac14 - \tfrac12\m$$
  2. STEP 2

    Set equal

    $$x^2 = \m x + \q$$
  3. STEP 3

    Standard form

    $$x^2 - \m x - \q = 0$$

    So \(a = 1\), \(b = -\m\), \(c = -\q\).

  4. STEP 4

    Discriminant = 0

    $$D = (-\m)^2 - 4\cdot 1\cdot(-\q) = \m^2 + 4\q = 0$$
  5. STEP 5

    Replace q and solve for m

    Now step 1 comes back: for q we put in \(\tfrac14 - \tfrac12\m\). Then only one unknown is left.

    $$\begin{aligned} \m^2 + 4\left(\tfrac14 - \tfrac12\m\right) &= 0 \\ \m^2 - 2\m + 1 &= 0 \\ (\m - 1)^2 &= 0 \iff \m = 1 \end{aligned}$$

    And \(\q = \tfrac14 - \tfrac12\cdot 1 = -\tfrac14\).

  6. STEP 6

    Write down the tangent

    $$t(x) = x - \tfrac14$$
  7. STEP 7

    Point of contact

    For free here: Q itself is the point of contact. Check: \(t(\tfrac12) = \tfrac12 - \tfrac14 = \tfrac14\) ✓

\(t(x) = x - \frac14\).

A4 (b)

General point \(P = (p, p^2)\) on the standard parabola \(f(x) = x^2\). Find the tangent \(t(x) = \m x + \q\) to f at the point P.
p is just a number we don't fix. Calculate exactly as in (a), only with p instead of ½.
  1. STEP 1

    Set-up: P lies on t

    As in (a): \(t(p) = p^2\), solve for q.

    $$\m p + \q = p^2 \iff \q = p^2 - \m p$$
  2. STEPS 2–4

    Set equal, standard form, D = 0

    Exactly as in (a), this doesn't change.

    $$x^2 - \m x - \q = 0, \qquad D = \m^2 + 4\q = 0$$
  3. STEP 5

    Replace q, solve for m

    Hint: at the end there's a binomial formula.

    $$\begin{aligned} \m^2 + 4(p^2 - \m p) &= 0 \\ \m^2 - 4p\,\m + 4p^2 &= 0 \\ (\m - 2p)^2 &= 0 \iff \m = 2p \end{aligned}$$

    And \(\q = p^2 - 2p\cdot p = -p^2\).

  4. STEP 6

    Tangent

    $$t(x) = 2p\cdot x - p^2$$
This is Proposition 17.2.4: slope = twice the x-coordinate, y-intercept = minus the y-coordinate. You need this shortcut in A5.

A5

Using Proposition 17.2.4: give the tangents to the standard parabola \(f(x) = x^2\) at the points \(A = (-2, 4)\), \(B = (3, 9)\), \(C = (1, 1)\), \(D = (\frac12, \frac14)\), \(E = (-\frac32, \frac94)\), \(O = (0, 0)\).
  1. RULE

    The shortcut

    p is the x-coordinate of the point. Substitute into \(t(x) = 2p\cdot x - p^2\). Careful with negative p: \((-2)^2 = 4\), so \(-p^2 = -4\).

  2. A

    \(A = (-2, 4)\), so \(p = -2\)

    $$t_A(x) = 2\cdot(-2)\cdot x - (-2)^2 = -4x - 4$$
  3. B

    \(B = (3, 9)\)

    $$t_B(x) = 6x - 9$$
  4. C

    \(C = (1, 1)\)

    $$t_C(x) = 2x - 1$$
  5. D

    \(D = (\frac12, \frac14)\)

    $$t_D(x) = x - \tfrac14 \quad\text{(same as in A4 (a)!)}$$
  6. E

    \(E = (-\frac32, \frac94)\)

    $$t_E(x) = 2\cdot\left(-\tfrac32\right) x - \tfrac94 = -3x - \tfrac94$$
  7. O

    \(O = (0, 0)\)

    $$t_O(x) = 0\cdot x - 0 = 0 \quad\text{(this is the x-axis)}$$
Sketch the standard parabola with all six tangents, or draw them in GeoGebra. Each line touches the parabola exactly at its point.

A6

(a) Find all tangents to the standard parabola \(f(x) = x^2\) that pass through \(A = (1, -3)\). A does not lie on the parabola.
(b) Give the points of contact.
Here the 2nd method from the script = our recipe. The 1st method is below.
  1. STEP 1

    Set-up: A lies on t

    \(t(1) = -3\). Solve for q.

    $$\m\cdot 1 + \q = -3 \iff \q = -\m - 3$$
  2. STEPS 2–3

    Set equal, standard form

    As in A4.

    $$x^2 = \m x + \q \iff x^2 - \m x - \q = 0$$
  3. STEP 4

    Discriminant = 0

    $$D = \m^2 + 4\q = 0$$
  4. STEP 5

    Replace q, solve for m

    $$\begin{aligned} \m^2 + 4(-\m - 3) &= 0 \\ \m^2 - 4\m - 12 &= 0 \\ \m_{1,2} &= \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 + 48}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm 8}{2} \\ \m_1 = 6, &\quad \m_2 = -2 \end{aligned}$$

    And \(\q_1 = -6 - 3 = -9\), \(\q_2 = 2 - 3 = -1\).

  5. STEP 6

    Tangents

    $$t_1(x) = 6x - 9, \qquad t_2(x) = -2x - 1$$
  6. STEP 7

    Points of contact (b)

    \(b = -\m\), so \(x = -\frac{b}{2a} = \frac{\m}{2}\). And y = x², because the point lies on the standard parabola.

    $$\m_1 = 6:\ x = 3,\ P_1 = (3, 9) \qquad \m_2 = -2:\ x = -1,\ P_2 = (-1, 1)$$
\(t_1(x) = 6x - 9\) touches at \((3, 9)\), \(t_2(x) = -2x - 1\) touches at \((-1, 1)\).

1st method (with the shortcut from A4 (b)): we need p with \(t_p(1) = -3\): \(2p - p^2 = -3 \iff p^2 - 2p - 3 = 0 \iff p = 3\) or \(p = -1\). Same tangents, same points of contact.

A7

(a) Find all tangents to \(f(x) = \frac14(x + 3)^2 + 1\) that pass through \(P = (-2, -1)\).
(b) Find each point of contact.
  1. STEP 1

    Set-up: P lies on t

    $$\m\cdot(-2) + \q = -1 \iff \q = 2\m - 1$$
  2. STEP 2

    Set equal

    $$\tfrac14(x + 3)^2 + 1 = \m x + \q$$
  3. STEP 3

    Standard form

    Hint: first multiply by 4, then the fraction is gone. \((x+3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9\) (binomial formula). Combine the x-terms as in A3.

    $$\begin{aligned} (x + 3)^2 + 4 &= 4\m x + 4\q \\ x^2 + 6x + 13 &= 4\m x + 4\q \\ x^2 + (6 - 4\m)\,x + (13 - 4\q) &= 0 \end{aligned}$$

    So \(a = 1\), \(b = 6 - 4\m\), \(c = 13 - 4\q\).

  4. STEP 4

    Discriminant = 0

    Expand, then divide everything by 4: smaller numbers.

    $$\begin{aligned} (6 - 4\m)^2 - 4(13 - 4\q) &= 0 \\ 36 - 48\m + 16\m^2 - 52 + 16\q &= 0 \quad\mid :4 \\ 4\m^2 - 12\m - 4 + 4\q &= 0 \end{aligned}$$
  5. STEP 5

    Replace q, solve for m

    $$\begin{aligned} 4\m^2 - 12\m - 4 + 4(2\m - 1) &= 0 \\ 4\m^2 - 4\m - 8 &= 0 \quad\mid :4 \\ \m^2 - \m - 2 &= 0 \\ \m_{1,2} = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 8}}{2} = \frac{1 \pm 3}{2}, &\quad \m_1 = 2,\ \m_2 = -1 \end{aligned}$$

    And \(\q_1 = 2\cdot 2 - 1 = 3\), \(\q_2 = 2\cdot(-1) - 1 = -3\).

  6. STEP 6

    Tangents

    $$t_1(x) = 2x + 3, \qquad t_2(x) = -x - 3$$
  7. STEP 7

    Points of contact (b)

    \(x = -\frac{b}{2a} = -\frac{6 - 4\m}{2} = 2\m - 3\), then calculate y with t.

    $$\begin{aligned} \m_1 = 2: &\quad x = 1,\quad y = t_1(1) = 5 \\ \m_2 = -1: &\quad x = -5,\quad y = t_2(-5) = 2 \end{aligned}$$

    Check: \(f(1) = \tfrac14\cdot 16 + 1 = 5\) ✓, \(f(-5) = \tfrac14\cdot 4 + 1 = 2\) ✓

\(t_1(x) = 2x + 3\) touches at \((1, 5)\), \(t_2(x) = -x - 3\) touches at \((-5, 2)\). (c) Drawing: vertex \(S = (-3, 1)\), stretch factor ¼. Check it in the picture (example “¼(x + 3)² + 1”).

A8

Find the tangent to \(f(x) = \frac14(x + 3)^2 + 1\) at the point \(A = (-1, f(-1))\).
  1. STEP 0

    Calculate the point

    f(−1) means: substitute −1 for x.

    $$f(-1) = \tfrac14(-1 + 3)^2 + 1 = \tfrac14\cdot 4 + 1 = 2, \qquad A = (-1, 2)$$
  2. STEP 1

    Set-up: A lies on t

    $$\m\cdot(-1) + \q = 2 \iff \q = \m + 2$$
  3. STEPS 2–4

    Set equal, standard form, D = 0

    Same parabola as in A7, so exactly the same working up to here.

    $$x^2 + (6 - 4\m)\,x + (13 - 4\q) = 0, \qquad 4\m^2 - 12\m - 4 + 4\q = 0$$
  4. STEP 5

    Replace q, solve for m

    $$\begin{aligned} 4\m^2 - 12\m - 4 + 4(\m + 2) &= 0 \\ 4\m^2 - 8\m + 4 &= 0 \quad\mid :4 \\ \m^2 - 2\m + 1 = (\m - 1)^2 &= 0 \iff \m = 1 \end{aligned}$$

    And \(\q = 1 + 2 = 3\). Only one m: A lies on the parabola, so there is only one tangent.

  5. STEP 6

    Tangent

    $$t(x) = x + 3$$
  6. STEP 7

    Check

    The point of contact must be A: \(x = 2\m - 3 = -1\) ✓ and \(t(-1) = 2\) ✓

\(t(x) = x + 3\), point of contact \(A = (-1, 2)\).

5 · Circles (A9–A12)

A circle is the set of all points at exactly distance r from a centre M. Pythagoras turns this into the circle equation:

$$(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2 \qquad M = (a, b),\ \text{radius } r$$

Careful with signs: \((y + 10)^2\) is \((y - (-10))^2\), so \(b = -10\).

For A9 you need completing the square. If that's rusty: Algebra toolbox.

Reading and writing the circle equation

The script writes the circle with centre M and radius r as \(K_r(M)\). For example, \(K_1(0, 0)\) is the circle around the origin with radius 1. Three traps when reading it off:

1. The right side is r², not r.

The radius is the square root of the right side.

$$x^2 + y^2 = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; r = \sqrt1 = 1$$

2. Flip the signs.

The bracket holds the centre with a minus. And the order inside a square doesn't matter.

$$\begin{aligned} (y + 7)^2 &= (y - (-7))^2 \;\Rightarrow\; b = -7 \\ (3 - x)^2 &= (x - 3)^2 \;\Rightarrow\; a = 3 \end{aligned}$$

3. Check the right side.

A square is never negative. So:

  • Positive: a circle
  • Zero: only the point M
  • Negative: no point at all

What does \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\) describe?

  1. Wo liegt der Mittelpunkt?Where is the centre?

    Vergleiche mit \((x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2\). Was steht hier anstelle von a und b?Compare with \((x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2\). What stands in place of a and b here?

    STEP 1

    Read off the centre

    The circle equation has the form \((x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2\). Here there is only \(x^2\) and \(y^2\): nothing is subtracted, so a and b are both 0.

    $$x^2 + y^2 = (x - 0)^2 + (y - 0)^2 \;\Rightarrow\; M = (0, 0)$$

  2. Wie gross ist der Radius?What is the radius?

    Rechts steht \(r^2\). Welche Zahl mal sich selbst gibt 1?The right side is \(r^2\). Which number times itself gives 1?

    STEP 2

    Radius: square root of the right side

    In the circle equation, the right side is \(r^2\). So you get the radius with the square root.

    $$r^2 = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; r = \sqrt1 = 1$$
The circle around \((0, 0)\) with radius 1: \(K_1(0, 0)\).

Question 2

What does \((3 - x)^2 + (y + 7)^2 = \sqrt5\) describe?

  1. Erste Koordinate des Mittelpunkts?First coordinate of the centre?

    Fast. \(3 - x\) ist dasselbe wie \(-(x - 3)\), und im Quadrat fällt das Minus weg.Close. \(3 - x\) is the same as \(-(x - 3)\), and squaring removes the minus.

    Schreib \((3 - x)^2\) als \((x - \ldots)^2\). Im Quadrat ist die Reihenfolge egal.Write \((3 - x)^2\) as \((x - \ldots)^2\). Squared, the order does not matter.

    STEP 1

    Turn the first bracket around

    The form has \(x - a\). \(3 - x\) is the opposite of \(x - 3\), and squared that makes no difference.

    $$(3 - x)^2 = \big(-(x - 3)\big)^2 = (x - 3)^2 \;\Rightarrow\; a = 3$$

  2. Zweite Koordinate des Mittelpunkts?Second coordinate of the centre?

    Achtung Vorzeichen: \(y + 7 = y - (-7)\).Watch the sign: \(y + 7 = y - (-7)\).

    Schreib \(y + 7\) als \(y - (\ldots)\).Write \(y + 7\) as \(y - (\ldots)\).

    STEP 2

    The second bracket

    The form has \(y - b\). Write the plus as minus a negative number: \(y + 7 = y - (-7)\).

    $$(y + 7)^2 = \big(y - (-7)\big)^2 \;\Rightarrow\; b = -7$$

  3. Wie gross ist der Radius?What is the radius?

    Rechts steht \(r^2 = \sqrt5\), nicht r. Zieh nochmals die Wurzel.The right side is \(r^2 = \sqrt5\), not r. Take the square root once more.

    Rechts steht \(\sqrt5\), nicht 5. Und das ist erst \(r^2\).The right side is \(\sqrt5\), not 5. And that is only \(r^2\).

    Rechts steht \(r^2\). Der Radius ist die Wurzel der rechten Seite. Tipp: √√5 darfst du so eintippen.The right side is \(r^2\). The radius is the square root of it. Tip: you may type √√5.

    STEP 3

    Radius: square root of the right side

    The right side is \(r^2\), here \(r^2 = \sqrt5\). The radius is its square root: the root of the root.

    $$r = \sqrt{\sqrt5} = \sqrt[4]{5} \approx 1.50$$
The circle around \((3, -7)\) with radius \(\sqrt[4]{5}\): \(K_{\sqrt[4]{5}}(3, -7)\). Remember: \(\sqrt5\) is \(r^2\), not the radius.

Question 3

What does \(x^2 + y^2 = 0\) describe?

  1. Dein Tipp, bevor du rechnest:Your guess, before you calculate:

    STEP 1

    Squares are never negative

    \(x^2 \ge 0\) and \(y^2 \ge 0\), for every number.

  2. Wann ist \(x^2 + y^2 = 0\)?When is \(x^2 + y^2 = 0\)?

    STEP 2

    When is the sum 0?

    Two numbers that are not negative only add up to 0 if both are 0.

    $$x^2 = 0 \text{ and } y^2 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 0,\ y = 0$$
Only one single point: \(K_0(0, 0) = \{(0, 0)\}\), a “circle” with radius 0.

Question 4

What does \(x^2 + y^2 = -1\) describe?

  1. Dein Tipp, bevor du rechnest:Your guess, before you calculate:

    STEP 1

    Squares are never negative

    So the left side is always at least 0.

    $$x^2 + y^2 \ge 0$$

  2. Kann \(x^2 + y^2\) gleich \(-1\) sein?Can \(x^2 + y^2\) equal \(-1\)?

    STEP 2

    Compare with the right side

    Something that is at least 0 can never be −1.

    $$x^2 + y^2 \ge 0 > -1$$
No point solves the equation: the empty set \(\emptyset\).

Backwards works the same way: circle with radius \(\sqrt3\) and centre \((2, -1)\):

$$(x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = (\sqrt3)^2 = 3$$

Recipe A: read off centre and radius

With the example \(x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 3 = 0\), step by step:

  1. Sort\((x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 4y) = 3\)x-terms together, y-terms together, the number to the right.
  2. Complete the square\(x^2 - 6x = (x - 3)^2 - 9\)\(y^2 + 4y = (y + 2)^2 - 4\)Half the number in front of x goes into the bracket; subtract its square again. Same for y.
  3. Put it in\((x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 3 + 9 + 4\)\((x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 16\)The 9 and 4 that were subtracted are added on the right. Now the equation has the circle form
    \((x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2\).
  4. Read off\(M = (3, -2)\)\(r = \sqrt{16} = 4\)Flip the signs from the brackets. If the right side is 0: just one point. If it is negative: no point at all.

Recipe B: intersect circle and line

With the example: circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\) and line \(x - y + 1 = 0\).

  1. Solve the line for y\(y = x + 1\)In general: \(y = \m x + \q\).
  2. Put it into the circle equation\(x^2 + (x + 1)^2 = 25\)Wherever y stands, write \((x + 1)\), with brackets.
  3. Standard form\(x^2 + x^2 + 2x + 1 = 25\)\(2x^2 + 2x - 24 = 0\)\(x^2 + x - 12 = 0\)Binomial formula, everything to the left, here also divide by 2. Goal: \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\).
  4. Quadratic formula\(D = 1^2 - 4\cdot 1\cdot(-12) = 49\)\(x = \dfrac{-1 \pm 7}{2}\)\(x_1 = 3,\ x_2 = -4\)D > 0: two intersection points.
    D = 0: one (tangent).
    D < 0: none. Just like with parabolas!

  6. Check\(3^2 + 4^2 = 25\ \checkmark\)\((-4)^2 + (-3)^2 = 25\ \checkmark\)Both points also lie on the line.

A9 (a) e)

Find the centre and radius of the circle \(x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 20y + 73 = 0\).
  1. STEP 1

    Sort

    $$(x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 20y) + 73 = 0$$
  2. STEP 2

    Complete the square for x

    The number in front of x is −6. Half of it is −3, which goes into the bracket. Subtract \((-3)^2 = 9\) again so nothing changes.

    $$x^2 - 6x = (x - 3)^2 - 9$$

    Check: \((x - 3)^2 = x^2 - 6x + 9\), minus 9 gives \(x^2 - 6x\) again ✓

  3. STEP 3

    Complete the square for y

    Half of 20 is 10, \(10^2 = 100\).

    $$y^2 + 20y = (y + 10)^2 - 100$$
  4. STEP 4

    Substitute, numbers to the right

    $$\begin{aligned} (x - 3)^2 - 9 + (y + 10)^2 - 100 + 73 &= 0 \\ (x - 3)^2 + (y + 10)^2 &= 36 \end{aligned}$$
  5. STEP 5

    Read off

    \((y + 10)^2 = (y - (-10))^2\), so \(b = -10\).

    $$M = (3, -10), \qquad r = \sqrt{36} = 6$$

A9 (a) a)–d), f) and (b)

(a) Find centre and radius:
a) \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\)
b) \(x^2 + y^2 - 5 = 0\)
c) \(x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2 - \frac14 = 0\)
d) \(x^2 + 2x + 1 + y^2 - \frac14 = 0\)
f) \(x^2 + y^2 - 2\sqrt3 x + 4\sqrt2 y + 4 = 0\)
(b) Why are these not circles? What are they?
a) \(x^2 + y^2 + 25 = 0\)
b) \(x^2 + y^2 = 0\)
c) \(x^2 + \left(\frac{y}{2}\right)^2 - 1 = 0\)
  1. a) b)

    Almost done already

    \(x^2 = (x - 0)^2\). The radius is the square root of the right-hand side.

    $$\text{a) } M = (0, 0),\ r = 5 \qquad \text{b) } x^2 + y^2 = 5:\ M = (0, 0),\ r = \sqrt5$$
  2. c) d)

    Spot the binomial formula

    \(x^2 - 2x + 1\) already is \((x - 1)^2\). No completing needed.

    $$\text{c) } (x - 1)^2 + y^2 = \tfrac14:\ M = (1, 0),\ r = \tfrac12 \qquad \text{d) } (x + 1)^2 + y^2 = \tfrac14:\ M = (-1, 0),\ r = \tfrac12$$
  3. f)

    Completing the square with roots

    Exactly like e). Half of \(-2\sqrt3\) is \(-\sqrt3\), square 3. Half of \(4\sqrt2\) is \(2\sqrt2\), square 8.

    $$\begin{aligned} (x - \sqrt3)^2 - 3 + (y + 2\sqrt2)^2 - 8 + 4 &= 0 \\ (x - \sqrt3)^2 + (y + 2\sqrt2)^2 &= 7 \end{aligned}$$

    \(M = (\sqrt3, -2\sqrt2)\), \(r = \sqrt7\).

  4. (b)

    When the right-hand side isn't positive

    Squares are never negative.

    • a) \(x^2 + y^2 = -25\): impossible, empty set.
    • b) \(x^2 + y^2 = 0\): only if x = 0 and y = 0, so just the point (0, 0).
    • c) \(x^2 + \left(\frac{y}{2}\right)^2 = 1\): a circle stretched to double in the y-direction, an ellipse with semi-axes 1 and 2.

A10

The zero sets of these functions are not circles, but simple shapes. Sketch them:
(a) \(y\)
(b) \(y - 3\)
(c) \(x\)
(d) \(x + 4\)
(e) \(x - y\)
(f) \(x + y\)
(g) \(\frac12 x - 3 - y\)
(h) \(2x + 3y - 6\)
(i) \(x^2 - y\)
(j) \(x - y^2\)
(k) \(xy\)
(l) \((x + 4)(y - 3)\)
(m) \(xy + 2x + 5y + 10\)
(n) \(0\)
(o) \(-2\)
(p) \(xy(x - y)\)
(q) \(x^3y - xy^3\); extra (r), (s).
  1. RULE

    Set it equal to zero, solve for y (or x)

    The zero set of f(x, y) is all points with f(x, y) = 0. If you can solve for y, you immediately see a familiar function.

  2. (a)–(d)

    Horizontal and vertical

    (a) \(y = 0\): x-axis · (b) \(y = 3\): horizontal line · (c) \(x = 0\): y-axis · (d) \(x = -4\): vertical line

  3. (e)–(h)

    Sloped lines

    (e) \(y = x\) · (f) \(y = -x\) · (g) \(y = \frac12 x - 3\) · (h) \(3y = -2x + 6 \iff y = -\frac23 x + 2\)

  4. (i)(j)

    Parabolas

    (i) \(y = x^2\): standard parabola · (j) \(x = y^2\): the same parabola, opening to the right

  5. (k)–(m)

    A product is zero when one factor is zero

    (k) \(xy = 0\): x = 0 or y = 0, so both axes · (l) \(x = -4\) or \(y = 3\): two lines · (m) factorise: \(xy + 2x + 5y + 10 = (x + 5)(y + 2)\), so \(x = -5\) or \(y = -2\)

  6. (n)(o)

    Special cases

    (n) \(0 = 0\) is always true: the whole plane · (o) \(-2 = 0\) is never true: empty set

  7. (p)(q)

    Several factors

    (p) \(x = 0\), \(y = 0\) or \(y = x\) · (q) \(x^3y - xy^3 = xy(x - y)(x + y)\): plus \(y = -x\)

  8. (r)(s)

    A graph as a zero set

    (r) \(y = x^3 \iff x^3 - y = 0\), so \(f(x, y) = x^3 - y\) · (s) in general \(f(x, y) = g(x) - y\)

A12

Find the intersection points of the circle around \((0, 0)\) with radius 85 and the circle around \((140, 20)\) with radius 65.
Here is a shorter way than in the script: subtract the equations. Then \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) cancel and a line is left, so it's A11 again.
  1. STEP 1

    Both circle equations

    $$\text{(I) } x^2 + y^2 = 7225 \qquad \text{(II) } (x - 140)^2 + (y - 20)^2 = 4225$$
  2. STEP 2

    Expand (II)

    $$x^2 - 280x + 19600 + y^2 - 40y + 400 = 4225$$
  3. STEP 3

    (I) minus (II)

    \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) cancel.

    $$\begin{aligned} 280x + 40y - 20000 &= 3000 \\ 280x + 40y &= 23000 \quad\mid :40 \\ 7x + y &= 575 \iff y = 575 - 7x \end{aligned}$$

    This is the line through both intersection points.

  4. STEP 4

    Now recipe B: substitute into (I)

    $$\begin{aligned} x^2 + (575 - 7x)^2 &= 7225 \\ 50x^2 - 8050x + 323400 &= 0 \quad\mid :50 \\ x^2 - 161x + 6468 &= 0,\quad x = \tfrac{161 \pm 7}{2} \end{aligned}$$

    \(x_1 = 84\), \(x_2 = 77\).

  5. STEP 5

    y from the line
